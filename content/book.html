Machine Code Mayhem

The book of how we make our games, by Daniel Vaughan with the Dalex Software team. Coming soon on Leanpub.

Everybody asks how a game fits into 48K. So the whole studio sat down and wrote it out: Machine Code Mayhem: Writing ZX Spectrum Games in Z80 Assembly, from Six Complete Games. It takes our six games apart, one idea at a time, until you can change them, rebuild them and start one of your own.

It is written for programmers who know a modern language and have never written a line of assembly. Every routine in it is copied from a game that builds, loads from tape and plays, and every chapter ends with things to try in an emulator.

What is in it

  • The machine and the language. Memory, the strange screen layout, frames and clock ticks, the 128K’s banks and its sound chip, then Z80 assembly explained for people who already program.
  • The engine room. The interrupt that keeps time, a main loop that never loses a key press, movement without fractions, drawing without flicker, and music on the AY, all from Mower Mayhem.
  • Six games, six lessons. A dictionary packed into a few kilobytes in Wordris, physics with whole numbers in Antigravity, a scrolling jungle in Tiger Gorilla, rooms full of people in Gold Master and fair timing in WOLF.
  • Making it good. How we test a machine-code game with recipes and bots, how Emeka playtests, how Derek signs a tape off, and how to start your own.
