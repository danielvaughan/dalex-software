About Dalex Software

Dalex Software is a small games house on three floors of a converted mill in Manchester. It is 1987, it is raining, and somebody has just put the kettle on.

We make games for the Sinclair ZX Spectrum: bright, fair, a little bit daft, and built to load first time. Every one is written for the real machine, with its eight colours, its 128K and its AY sound chip, and every one goes out in a proper cassette box with an inlay you can read.

How a game gets made

Every Dalex game goes through the same seven stages, and the board on the Production wall shows where each one is. Watch the film of the studio at work.

  1. Design
    Malcolm writes the brief and Colin turns it into rules: what you do, what gets in your way, and why you will want one more go.
  2. Assets
    Neil, Hyun-joo and Simon draw the sprites, screens and loading picture; Dez writes the tunes and the bleeps.
  3. Build
    Gareth puts it all together in machine code and saves it to tape.
  4. Verify
    Vik loads the tape from cold and checks every point on the list. If it doesn’t load, it doesn’t go.
  5. Playtest
    Emeka plays it, notebook open. The findings go back to Gareth until there are none that matter.
  6. Sign-off
    Derek reads the test report and the notebook and signs the gate sheet, or doesn’t.
  7. Release
    The gold master goes out with Omnibot, the cassettes get their inlays, and a copy goes over the courtyard to INK & PAPER.
